# Design

Agreed design. Workers read this before building. Winning and charters come later; the current focus is the player experience.

## Core idea
You are a station administrator. Independent ship captains act on their own. You build structures, hire captains, and post paid contracts. Captains decide for themselves whether a contract is worth it.

## Resources
- Credits: you pay contracts and hires with them. They come in from selling ore to the Factory, from the station fee share of every payment to a captain (20 to 30%), from the Outfitter's share of module sales (35%) and ship sales, and a small corporate grant (30 cr/min).
- Ore, by type: iron (Cinder Belt), titanium (Talon Reach), crystal (Crystal Veil). Miners deliver the type they mined; salvage is reprocessed at the Salvage Yard into mostly iron, some titanium and a little crystal, which you buy from the yard. Ore pays for buildings (mostly iron, advanced ones some titanium) and Factory tooling, and is what you sell. Rarer ore is worth much more per unit: crystal >> titanium >> iron.
- Credit flow: you pay a miner per load, the load is ore, you sell the ore to the Factory. A fair-pay iron load is slightly profitable, titanium with escorts more so, crystal the most but only with good gear and escorts. Overpaying or losing ships costs you.

## Buildings (near the Home Station, inside the build zone)
- Mining Outpost: hires miners (200 cr). Max 4. 500 cr + 100 iron.
- Mercenary Outpost: hires mercenaries (300 cr). Max 4. 600 cr + 120 iron + 30 titanium.
- Station addons: the Home Station core has 6 fixed addon bays. Addons are built onto a free bay (pick the bay in the build view); one of each type. Factory and Outfitter are addons; a Science Lab addon (research) comes later.
- Factory: the default addon, in bay 1 from the start; cannot be built again. It buys your ore and makes the modules captains buy at the Outfitter.
  - Market: one ore stock per type with a target level. Demand (low, normal, high) and the price it is willing to pay rise when its stock is low and fall when it is high, between a floor and a ceiling; open backorders raise demand for the ore they need. The stock drains by production and a steady corporate export, so prices recover.
  - Selling: auto-sell is on from the start. Each ore's ask follows the market by default (95% of what the Factory pays, shown as "auto"); +/- sets it by hand, "=" follows again. You set an asking price per ore type; the closer to (or below) what the Factory pays, the faster it buys (full pace at 80% of its price, nothing past 135%). It never sells below your "keep at least" reserve per type. The panel shows the expected sales rate; the top bar shows each ore with a small demand meter.
  - Production: Mk I from iron, Mk II from titanium plus iron, Mk III from crystal plus titanium, one at a time (40 / 75 / 120 s). Backorders first, then a spread of parts for the roles and ranks you hired. Warehouse of 16 parts. Mk II and Mk III need tooling upgrades (250 iron + 50 titanium, then 200 iron + 400 titanium + 200 crystal) and that ore in the Factory's stock. Rare salvaged parts go into the warehouse and let it make that module early, or discount the next one.
  - The trade-off: sell rare ore for credits now, or let it feed production so captains get better gear and take on more dangerous belts.
- Outfitter (station addon, 800 cr, 200 iron + 50 titanium, max 1): captains buy the Factory's modules here with their own earnings. Stock is finite. Out of stock: Bold takes another part or backorders, Cautious and Greedy backorder and wait, Lazy takes whatever is there or tries again later. No Outfitter, no module sales.
- Salvage Yard: hires salvagers (250 cr). Max 3. 500 cr + 80 iron + 20 titanium. Runs as a small business: salvagers strip scrap fields on their own (free salvage, no contract needed), the yard pays them 40 cr per load from its own funds (starts with 300 cr; when short it owes the rest and pays debts first from sales), reprocesses 1 scrap per second into ore (75% iron, 20% titanium, 5% crystal; den ruins 60/28/12) and sells it to you at 70% of the Factory's current bid; you pay at once and the yard's shuttles fly the bought ore to the station (it counts as yours when it lands, about a minute or two later). The yard panel shows it on the way ("In transit: 40 iron (shuttle leaving in 0:12)", "On the way to the station: 31 iron") and a feed line says when it landed; with Factory auto-sell on, what you hold above your keep-at-least is sold on. Auto-buy (on from the start) buys it all while you keep 200 cr. You sell it on to the Factory: the gap is your margin. A posted salvage contract is an optional bonus per load on top of the yard's pay, to draw salvagers to one wreck or make a dangerous one worth it.
- Station complex: no corridors. Structures stand free in the build zone, 25 m clear of every other footprint, outside the station's keep-outs (100 m from the centre plus the addon bay and solar wing strips), with their docking lanes clear of the keep-outs and of other footprints. The docking mouth faces away from the station.
- Cargo shuttles: small captainless ships carry the goods to the station instead of pipes. Each Mining Outpost and the Salvage Yard runs 1 shuttle from the start and up to 3 as its throughput grows (60 units a trip). Miners unload into the outpost's stock; a shuttle takes a load (when it fills, or after 40 s, so small amounts never wait for a full load), flies to a station docking pad, and the ore counts as yours when it lands. Stock waiting 90 s with every shuttle away brings in one more shuttle. The top bar shows ore on its way (waiting or aboard) as a small "+40" after each ore. It flies back with supplies (cosmetic). The Mercenary Outpost has one shuttle on a cosmetic supply run every 90 s. The outpost panel shows "Stock waiting: 37 iron, shuttles 2/3". Raiders can shoot shuttles while they fly in open space (docked, loading and on the pads they are safe); a destroyed shuttle loses its load and the outpost sends a new one. While the station is disabled its pads are closed and shuttles wait at the approach point.
- Structure hulls: the Mining Outpost, Mercenary Outpost and Salvage Yard have 1600 hull, the Home Station 5000 (StructureHull). Raiders shoot them; at 0 a structure is disabled, never destroyed: an outpost stops its shuttles and hiring, the station closes its docking pads. Disabled structures are not shot any more (raiders loot instead, as before). The station repairs them like the turrets (from 8 s after the last hit, 1.2% of max hull per second, you pay about 10 cr per 100 hull) and they come back online at 40% hull. The building panel status shows the hull.
- Defense Turret (250 cr + 60 iron + 15 titanium, 15 s, max 8): standalone, anywhere in the build zone clear of footprints and docking lanes (18 m gap) (good for perimeter cover; the placement ghost shows its 900 m range). Shoots hostiles within 900 m on its own, raiders and ships attacking the colony first; leads its aim, 13 damage x 2.6/s at 72% accuracy, about 24 damage/s (about two thirds of a stock mercenary). Hold-fire toggle. Hull 650 + 150 shields; raiders can destroy it (it leaves scrap and can be rebuilt). The station repairs its hull slowly once it has not been hit for 8 s, and you pay RepairBay's rate less the station's 35% share (about 10 cr per 100 hull).
- Shield Generator (600 cr + 150 iron + 60 titanium, 30 s, max 1): projects a flattened shield bubble over the whole complex (radius fits the station and every structure plus 70 m, grows when the complex grows; standalone turrets do not stretch it). Ships fly through it. Hostile shots from outside hit the bubble instead of anything inside; hostiles already inside hit normally; friendly shots pass out. 2400 HP, regenerates 30 HP/s after 5 s without hits; at 0 it collapses and re-raises 45 s later at 40%. The generator (hull 900 + 150 shields) can only be shot while the bubble is down; destroyed, the bubble is gone until it is rebuilt.
- The build tooltip and the building panel of both show what they do in numbers (DefenseStats, computed from the scripts and the faction loadouts: damage per second, range against raider gun range, time to kill each raider type, time for 2 or 4 scavengers to break the bubble).

## Contracts
- Mining: on a belt, credits per delivered load.
- Bounty: on a raider ship or a hostile base (Scavenger Hideout, Corsair Nest, Syndicate Fortress), paid on destruction. The suggested pay and the bounty's danger follow the faction. Posting reserves the reward: it leaves your credits at once into an escrow held by the contract board (you cannot post more than you have; the top bar credits tooltip and the overview show "Reserved for bounties"). The payout comes from the escrow (split by rank and damage as before); anything left after the kill (nobody eligible) comes back. Cancel: all of it back while nobody has taken it; once taken, half back and half paid to the takers as compensation, split evenly (the cancel tooltip says which). A raider slipping home without dying refunds it all.
- Paid per delivery or trip (mining, escort, salvage bonus): nothing is reserved, but you can only post one while you can pay one payment. While the station cannot pay one payment the contract is paused ("Station can't pay"): captains do not take it until credits come in; a trip already under way is paid what is there and the feed says so.
- Escort: on a miner (or a mining contract), paid to escorts per completed trip. Like Majesty's protect flag.
- Later: Salvage (on a scrap field), Explore.

## Defence zones
- The player places up to 3 defence zones ("Defence zone" in the build bar, ZONES group; free). A zone is a centre and a radius (0.8 to 4 km, 200 m steps), drawn as a faint disc with a thin rim (cyan, gold with a kill bounty, pulsing red while raiders are inside) and a ring on the minimap. A centre within 5 km of the station moves with the station on its orbit; farther out it stays fixed in space (over a belt or a lane). Zones are areas ships fly through, so the station keep-outs and structure gaps do not apply; only the limit and the system edge do. Click its label or rim to select it.
- Panel: position, distance from home, mercenaries patrolling it, raiders inside, the radius stepper, the kill bounty (0 to 300 cr per raider killed inside, 10 cr steps) and its budget ("Add 100 cr", "Add 500 cr", "Take back"), what it paid so far, and "Remove zone".
- Kill bounty: paid from the zone's budget. The budget is reserved from your credits like a bounty (it cannot exceed your credits; the top bar and the overview show it as "zone budgets"); what is left comes back when you take it back or remove the zone. A raider killed inside pays the mercenaries who hit it in the last 90 s (and the killer), split evenly, while the budget lasts; turret kills pay nothing. Each payment is a feed line, a toast and a captain log entry; a feed line says when the budget is spent.
- Mercenaries: while any zone exists, idle mercs patrol more often (+20%, +10% more while one pays a kill bounty) and pick where: home 0.6 (Cautious 0.9, Lazy 1.2) against each zone 1, plus its kill bounty (per 40 cr +1, times 1 + Greed/2; Greedy x2, Cautious x0.6, Lazy x0.5), plus 1 (Bold 2) while raiders are inside; zones past 6 km from home: Cautious x0.5, Lazy x0.4, Bold x1.2. The decision log has the weights. In a zone they roam random points inside it, 1.5x as long as a home patrol. Raiders inside a zone within 2.5 km of a merc (or of its outpost, docked) count as threats: patrols engage them, docked mercs scramble. A feed line says when raiders enter a zone.

## Colony alarm
- When a structure (outposts, turrets, the shield generator, the station), the shield bubble or a cargo shuttle is shot, the colony raises the alarm for 25 s after the last hit (ColonyAlarm, the mirror of a raider base's alarm). Every 5 s while it lasts, the free mercenaries within 5 km (docked, resting, repairing with hull enough to scramble, or on patrol / home defence; not bounty hunters or escorts, not those going home for repairs) fly to it, circle it and fight its attackers until the alarm is over. One feed line per alarm says who answered ("Mining Outpost 1 is under attack: Bram Petrov, Kenji Valdez answering"); each merc logs why (attacker, distance, hull).

## Enemies
- Factions (data/factions/*.tres, FactionDef): each hostile base (DenDef) belongs to one. A faction sets its ship model and stats, behaviour (prey, aggression, flee threshold, steal or kill, packs, raid style), base model and toughness, loot and bounty multipliers, colour and names. All of them are hostile to the player and never fight each other. Belt danger comes from nearby bases.
- Scavengers: junk raider ships from Scavenger Hideouts (bases dug into asteroids) near the richer belts. They hunt miners, steal cargo, and raid near home now and then.
- Corsairs (outer system, Wraith Drift): fast light raiders from Corsair Nests. They hunt miners and salvagers in pairs over a wide arc, grab cargo and run early. They raid the colony rarely, in pairs, and not before 25 minutes.
- Syndicate (outer system, Wraith Drift): slow, tough gunships from a Syndicate Fortress (twice a big hideout's hull, regenerating shields, its own guns). They patrol their territory, engage mercenaries there and fight to the end; their rare raids (3 to 5 gunships, not before 40 minutes) go for the turrets and the outposts, not the ships. A fortress needs a geared fleet.
- Base defence: a base hit by the player's side raises the alarm ("Rustjaw Hideout is calling its raiders home") for 25 s after the last hit. Its ships within 14 km and same-faction ships of other bases within 6 km come back and fight the attackers (not those already fleeing home with loot); while the alarm lasts it launches below its cap 1.5x faster and every new ship joins the defence. Then they go back to roaming.
- Balance targets (measured in probes): a stock mercenary beats a scavenger 1v1 nearly always; corsairs dodge and run, a third or more get away from a lone merc; a syndicate gunship beats or drives off one stock merc more often than not and loses to two. Rustjaw Hideout falls to 3 stock mercs in about 2 to 3 min, Ashfang Hideout to 3 Mk I mercs, a Corsair Nest to 3 or 4 Mk I mercs (4 to 5 min, often one lost), the Syndicate Fortress to 5 or 6 mercs with some Mk II gear in about 3 to 5 min, with losses and failed runs possible. Faction toughness lives in data/factions/*.tres (FactionDef: base hull, shields, regen and guns, ship accuracy and dodge) and the DenDefs in data/systems/home_system.tres.
- Threat escalation (ThreatDirector, DenDef arrival fields): the game starts with the Rustjaw Hideout only. Then, one at a time: Ashfang Hideout (12 min), Redwake Corsair Nest (25 min, introduces the Corsairs), Hollowstar Corsair Nest (40 min), Obsidian Syndicate Fortress (60 min). A base comes at its time only once the colony has grown (Ashfang 3 captains and 3 structures, Redwake 5/4, Hollowstar 7/5, Obsidian 9/6), and anyway at 1.5x its time; at least 3 min between arrivals. While every base is destroyed the threat clock runs 1.5x. 75 s ahead a "Long-range scans" warning names the faction and belt and the spot shows as "Arriving" with a countdown (hologram, map, overview); the base warps in with the construction look and starts launching. A faction's first base posts a one-time intro. Bases that have not arrived add no belt danger, bounties, markers or raids; a new base waits 4 min before raiding. The overview gives a vague "Next: unknown signals, ~6 min".
- Raids (RaidDirector): the first at 5 min, then every 330 s falling to 170 s (+-40 s) as the pressure grows (half from game time, full at 45 min; half from colony size, captains plus structures, full at 20). Waves: 1 raider, plus 1 per 12 min, plus 1 per 6 of colony size past 4, up to 6; the first raid has 1 raider, the second at most 2. Faction raid sizes as before (Corsairs a pair, Syndicate 3 to 5). Each raid has an objective, by weight: an outpost 3 (Syndicate 4.5), the cargo shuttles of an outpost 1.5 (3 while one is loaded in flight; not the Syndicate), the station 1 after the first 2 raids (Syndicate 2), turrets 1 for the Syndicate; disabled structures are skipped. 30 s ahead a feed line and a toast warn: "Raid incoming from Rustjaw Hideout toward Mining Outpost 1: 2 scavengers, launching in 30 s"; a base destroyed meanwhile calls it off. Measured in a 50 min run with a colony growing from 8 to 16: 13 raids (the old director sent about 8), at 5, 10, 15, 18, 22, 26, 30 min and then about every 3 to 4 min; 1, 2, 3, 3, 3, 4, 5 raiders, 6 from 40 min.
- Raiders attack in order: the shield bubble while it is up (they never chase targets inside it from outside), then the defense turrets, then the shield generator, then the raid's objective (a structure's hull, the shuttle), then shuttles and ships near home (the Syndicate shoots every structure's hull instead); with nothing left to shoot they loot credits from a structure (15 cr per 8 s each, up to 75, Syndicate double). They flee home below 35% hull.
- Raid outcomes on a Mining Outpost (probe, 3 runs each; "held" = the outpost never disabled):

| Raid | Nothing | 2 turrets + shield (1100 cr) | 2 stock mercs (1200 cr) | Both |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 scavenger | disabled, 75 cr looted | held, 0 lost | held | |
| 2 scavengers | disabled, 150 cr | held | held | |
| 3 scavengers | disabled, 225 cr | held, bubble holds | held | |
| 4 scavengers | disabled, 300 cr | held, bubble holds | held | |
| 6 scavengers | disabled, 450 cr | held, bubble breaks | held | held |
| 2 corsairs | disabled, 150 cr | held | held | |
| 3 syndicate | disabled, 360 cr | fails 3/3: both turrets lost | fails 2/3 | held 3/3, no losses |
| 5 syndicate | disabled, 710 cr | fails, turrets lost | fails | held 3/3, 2 turrets lost in 9 |

  Defences drive scavengers and corsairs off (they flee at 35% hull, so turrets kill few: 2 of 9 in a 3-raider raid) without losses, while mercenaries kill more but take damage and may be away. The Syndicate needs both.
- Later: the Swarm, rogue drones.

## Captains
The captain is the person. The ship is the hardware.
- Personality (headline): Bold, Cautious, Greedy, Lazy. It sets the starting attributes.
- Attributes 1 to 10, rolled at hire: Nerve, Greed, Diligence, Piloting, specialty skill (Mining or Gunnery), Loyalty.
- Traits (stage 3), earned from events, 2 to 3 max, some fade: Survivor, Shaken, Crystal Hound, Grudge, Lone Wolf, Union Rep.
- Reputation ranks (stage 3) instead of levels: Rookie, Licensed, Veteran, Ace, Legend. A rank unlocks module slots (2 up to 6) and higher module tiers. It does not add raw stats.
- Ship modules (stage 3), made by the Factory and bought by the captain with their own earnings at your Outfitter. Better defense and engine modules make miners take more dangerous belts (every 3 module tiers there count as one danger level less, at most 2). Personality decides what they buy. Slots: tool (drill / cannons / missiles), cargo or ammo, defense (armor, shields), engines, sensors.
- Career switching (stage 3): a captain can change role, e.g. a veteran Bold miner becomes a mercenary. They need a new ship; experience and traits carry over, the specialty skill starts low.
- Death: a destroyed ship has an eject chance (Piloting, modules). Ejected captains return without the ship and keep their rank. Otherwise they are gone for good.
- Mercenaries defend the area around home on their own, patrol the defence zones, answer the colony alarm within 5 km, take bounty and escort contracts by pay and personality, and go home to repair when damaged. Miners flee when attacked.
- Repairs: hull never regenerates in space (shields do). Docked, a ship is repaired at a cost per hull point paid from the captain's wallet (a full merc repair is about 55 cr); the station keeps 35% as income. Broke captains repair what they can pay for. After a fight a merc goes home to repair below its personality's level (Cautious at almost any damage, Bold and Greedy later, Lazy early); escorts and fleets finish their job first.
- Attribute Teamwork (1 to 10, personality base +-2; Lone Wolf -3, Union Rep +2). A bounty too strong for one ship (hideouts, groups) makes a merc call a fleet of 2 to 6: as many as it takes for the free mercs' combined power to cover 80% of the target's need (hull, shields, guns and the ships it can call home). Too few free mercs: the caller refuses ("Needs about 5 mercenaries, 3 available"); even 6 mercs like the hired ones too weak: "Too dangerous for the current fleet". The target panel shows the same estimate and why mercs refused, and the decision log has the numbers. Others join by Teamwork, personality, share, Grudge, distance and how far the fleet covers the need (more likely when they would complete it; a no is asked again after 40 s); it forms up off the station, sets out once strong enough (or gives up after 2 min still too weak), flies in formation, focuses fire and retreats together; the leader is picked by rank, then Nerve. The bounty is split by rank and damage done, less for those who left early. Lone wolves (high Nerve, low Teamwork) take such bounties alone, keep it all, and die more often.

## Salvage
Destroyed ships (any side) leave scrap fields. Stage 2: scrap fields exist and fade after a while. Stage 3: the salvager role, free salvage paid by the Salvage Yard, optional Salvage contracts as a bonus; scrap is reprocessed into ore the yard sells you, and sometimes gives rare parts.

## UI
- Look ("Industrial Expanse"): dark gunmetal panels with cut corners, hairline borders, a short amber accent stripe, faint scanlines. One theme for the HUD, the pause menu and the main menu: ui/theme.tres, generated from scripts/ui/theme/hud_theme_builder.gd (palette and fonts in hud_style.gd, panel style ChamferBox). Regenerate after a change: `Godot --headless --path . --script res://scripts/ui/theme/generate_theme.gd`. Fonts: Barlow and Barlow Condensed (SIL OFL, assets/ui/fonts), tabular figures for numbers that tick. Small icons: monochrome SVGs in assets/ui/icons, tinted in code. Build bar icons: rendered PNGs in assets/ui/icons/build/<id>.png (blender/render_icons.py), a drawn placeholder while one is missing.
- Top bar (full width): system name; credits and each ore with icon, delta flashes, cargo on the way and the Factory demand meter; the shield bubble; sim time and speed. Tooltips as before.
- Left panel, tabbed (Tab collapses it; the top bar HOME button or H flies the camera to the home station), with live counts: ALL (default: contracts, threats and the system in one scrolling view), SYSTEM (star, planets, belts, structures, gates, scrap), CONTRACTS (posted contracts, reserved escrow), THREATS (bases by faction, their ships, the arrival outlook; red while raiders are out).
- Right column: the minimap, under it the pilot list, the selection panels at the bottom. An open selection panel is capped to the room under the minimap (its content scrolls) and the pilot list ends above it (only its title row when there is no room), so nothing overlaps.
- Pilot list: floats over space (no panel, outlined text). A title row (count, "in combat", collapse) over a rule line, a small summary line (combat, damaged, out, home), small role chips and the sort button (role, status, hull, wallet, rank). One row per pilot: small role badge, name, state icon and short state text (refusal reason while docked, work %), tiny hull over shield bar, wallet dim on the right. In combat or just hit: red pulsing left tick and reddish name; damaged: amber tick and name. Fleets under a fleet line in role order. Click selects, double click also centres the camera; the list scrolls to the selected pilot. Rows are pooled and refreshed 4 times a second.
- Contract list, bottom left above the event feed (the left panel ends above both): floats like the pilot list, one row per posted contract (type icon, target, reward or budget, takers or the bounty's fleet, "paused, station can't pay"), the reserved escrow in its header, at most 6 rows ("+N more" opens the CONTRACTS tab). Click flies to the contract and selects its target; hovering a row shows its cancel button. The CONTRACTS tab keeps the full list.
- Build bar: square tiles grouped ECONOMY, DEFENCE (with the defence zone tool), ADDONS; hotkeys F1 to F7; built count against the limit; compact cost, short resources in red; dimmed when unaffordable, a lock at the limit or with no free bay, lit while placing. Hovering shows the info card: cost against what you have, build time, hire terms, the DefenseStats numbers and why it cannot be built.
- Event feed: icon and coloured edge per kind, slides in, fades. Toasts are banners: red and held 5 s for raids, amber for scans and new factions, green otherwise.

## Stages
1. Contracts, miner AI, mining loop. (done)
2. Mercenary Outpost, mercenaries, scavengers and hideouts, combat, Bounty and Escort contracts, captain attributes plus the module-slot data model, scrap fields from destroyed ships. (done)
3. Reputation ranks, traits from events, Outfitter and captains buying modules, career switching, salvager role and Salvage contracts, death and ejection. (done)
3b. Factory and typed ore: iron, titanium and crystal; the Factory buys ore at a floating price (asking price, auto-sell, reserve) and makes the modules the Outfitter sells; tooling gates Mk II/III. (done)
4. Later: charters (win/lose), the cluster map and more systems.
