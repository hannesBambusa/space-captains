# Design

Agreed design. Workers read this before building. Winning and charters come later; the current focus is the player experience.

## Core idea
You are a station administrator. Independent ship captains act on their own. You build structures, hire captains, and post paid contracts. Captains decide for themselves whether a contract is worth it.

## Resources
- Credits: you pay contracts and hires with them. They come in from selling ore to the Factory, from the station fee share of every payment to a captain (20 to 30%), from the Outfitter's share of module sales (35%) and ship sales, and a corporate grant (90 cr/min, paid every 20 s).
- Ore, by type: iron, titanium and crystal, mined from asteroid clusters (see Asteroid clusters; the start: iron in Cinder Cluster, titanium in Talon Reach, crystal in Crystal Veil and Wraith Drift). Miners deliver the type they mined; salvage is reprocessed at the Salvage Yard into mostly iron, some titanium and a little crystal, which you buy from the yard. Ore pays for buildings (mostly iron, advanced ones some titanium) and Factory tooling, and is what you sell. Rarer ore is worth much more per unit: crystal >> titanium >> iron.
- Credit flow: you pay a miner per load, the load is ore, you sell the ore to the Factory. A fair-pay iron load is slightly profitable, titanium with escorts more so, crystal the most but only with good gear and escorts. Overpaying or losing ships costs you.
- Planet goods (phase C2), brought back from planet raids: fuel (gas giants), water (ice and ocean worlds), rare metals (lava worlds), silicates (desert), heavy ore (rocky). They are Economy.Ore types 3 to 7 after the asteroid ore, so every ore array (stock, cargo depots, shuttles, the Factory market) holds eight; older saves are padded. All sell to the Factory (price floor to ceiling per unit: fuel 6 to 22, water 3 to 10, rare metals 12 to 38, silicates 4 to 14, heavy ore 3 to 12; targets 60 / 120 / 40 / 80 / 100; keep at least 20 of each planet good by default, so there is always some to refine and build with; older saves keep their own settings). Uses: every Mk III module needs 4 rare metals, Mk III drives (ion drive, combat thrusters) 6 fuel (Mk II drives need none), Mk III shields (shield projector, deflector) 8 silicates (ShipModule.build_ore, in the Factory's stock); the Factory refines 10 heavy ore into 12 iron and 4 titanium and 10 silicates into 4 crystal (Refine on the market tab, Shift: ten batches); water is kept for a later colony (sell it for now).

## Buildings (near the Home Station, inside the build zone)
- Mining Outpost: hires miners (200 cr). Max 4. 500 cr + 100 iron.
- Mercenary Outpost: hires mercenaries (300 cr). Max 4. 600 cr + 120 iron + 30 titanium.
- Station addons: the Home Station core has 6 fixed addon bays. Addons are built onto a free bay (pick the bay in the build view); one of each type. Factory, Outfitter and Science Lab are addons.
- Factory: the default addon, in bay 1 from the start; cannot be built again. It buys your ore and makes the modules captains buy at the Outfitter.
  - Market: one ore stock per type with a target level. Demand (low, normal, high) and the price it is willing to pay rise when its stock is low and fall when it is high, between a floor and a ceiling; open backorders raise demand for the ore they need. The stock drains by production and a steady corporate export, so prices recover.
  - Selling: auto-sell is on from the start. Each ore's ask follows the market by default (95% of what the Factory pays, shown as "auto"); +/- sets it by hand, "=" follows again. You set an asking price per ore type; the closer to (or below) what the Factory pays, the faster it buys (full pace at 80% of its price, nothing past 135%). It never sells below your "keep at least" reserve per type (the reserve only stops selling: building, research and tooling still spend that ore; the Keep tooltip says so, and the rate reads "keeping all" while nothing is above it), and never sells ore bought from the Salvage Yard (held back on top of the reserve, FactoryMarket.held: it shrinks as you spend or sell ore, "Sell now" still works). The panel shows the expected sales rate; the top bar shows each ore with a small demand meter.
  - Production (only researched modules, see Research): Mk I from iron, Mk II from titanium plus iron, Mk III from crystal plus titanium, one at a time (40 / 75 / 120 s). Backorders first, then a spread of parts for the roles and ranks you hired. Warehouse of 16 parts. Mk II and Mk III need tooling upgrades (250 iron + 50 titanium, then 200 iron + 400 titanium + 200 crystal) and that ore in the Factory's stock. Rare salvaged parts go into the warehouse and let it make that module early, or discount the next one.
  - The trade-off: sell rare ore for credits now, or let it feed production so captains get better gear and take on more dangerous clusters.
- Outfitter (station addon, 800 cr, 200 iron + 50 titanium, max 1): captains buy the Factory's modules here with their own earnings. Only researched modules are offered (a salvaged rare part in the warehouse sells anyway); the panel shows how many are researched, hover for the list with their numbers. Stock is finite. Out of stock: Bold takes another part or backorders, Cautious and Greedy backorder and wait, Lazy takes whatever is there or tries again later. No Outfitter, no module sales.
- Salvage Yard (model assets/models/buildings/salvage_yard.glb; its unload arm, conveyor and silo play on every salvage delivery): hires salvagers (250 cr). Max 3. 500 cr + 80 iron + 20 titanium. Runs as a small business: salvagers strip scrap fields on their own (free salvage, no contract needed), the yard pays them 40 cr per load from its own funds (starts with 300 cr; when short it owes the rest and pays debts first from sales), reprocesses 1 scrap per second into ore (75% iron, 20% titanium, 5% crystal; den ruins 60/28/12) and sells it to you at 70% of the Factory's current bid; you pay at once and the yard's shuttles fly the bought ore to the station (it counts as yours when it lands, about a minute or two later). The yard panel shows it on the way ("In transit: 40 iron (shuttle leaving in 0:12)", "On the way to the station: 31 iron") and a feed line says when it landed; Factory auto-sell never sells that bought ore: it is held back (on top of your keep-at-least) until you spend or sell it by hand, and the yard panel says so. Auto-buy (on from the start) buys it all while you keep 200 cr. A posted salvage contract is an optional bonus per load on top of the yard's pay, to draw salvagers to one wreck or make a dangerous one worth it.
- Station complex: no corridors. Structures stand free in the build zone, 25 m clear of every other footprint, outside the station's keep-outs (100 m from the centre plus the addon bay and solar wing strips), with their docking lanes clear of the keep-outs and of other footprints. The docking mouth faces away from the station.
- Cargo shuttles: small captainless ships carry the goods to the station instead of pipes. Each Mining Outpost and the Salvage Yard runs 1 shuttle from the start and up to 3 as its throughput grows (60 units a trip). Miners unload into the outpost's stock; a shuttle takes a load (when it fills, or after 40 s, so small amounts never wait for a full load), flies a short hop in the station's structures frame (straight, bent round the hub and other structures; no cruise arcs, no warp) to the nearest station docking pad (or the other one when it is free and that beats waiting), and the ore counts as yours when it lands. Stock waiting 90 s with every shuttle away brings in one more shuttle. The top bar shows ore on its way (waiting or aboard) as a small "+40" after each ore. It flies back with supplies (cosmetic). The Mercenary Outpost has one shuttle on a cosmetic supply run every 90 s. The outpost panel shows "Stock waiting: 37 iron, shuttles 2/3". Raiders can shoot shuttles while they fly between outpost and pads (docked, loading and on the pads they are safe); a destroyed shuttle loses its load and the outpost sends a new one after 45 s (the outpost panel shows "Replacement shuttle in 0:32"). While the station is disabled its pads are closed and shuttles hold beside the approach point.
- Structure hulls: the Mining Outpost, Mercenary Outpost and Salvage Yard have 1600 hull, the Home Station 5000 (StructureHull). Raiders shoot them; at 0 a structure is disabled, never destroyed: an outpost stops its shuttles and hiring, the station closes its docking pads. Disabled structures are not shot any more (raiders loot instead, as before). The station repairs them like the turrets (from 8 s after the last hit, 1.2% of max hull per second, you pay about 10 cr per 100 hull) and they come back online at 40% hull. The building panel status shows the hull.
- Defense Turret (250 cr + 60 iron + 15 titanium, 15 s, max 8): standalone, anywhere in the build zone clear of footprints and docking lanes (18 m gap) (good for perimeter cover; the placement ghost shows its 900 m range). Shoots hostiles within 900 m on its own, raiders and ships attacking the colony first; leads its aim, 13 damage x 2.6/s at 72% accuracy, about 24 damage/s (about two thirds of a stock mercenary). Hold-fire toggle. Hull 650 + 150 shields; raiders can destroy it (it leaves scrap and can be rebuilt). The station repairs its hull slowly once it has not been hit for 8 s, and you pay RepairBay's rate less the station's 35% share (about 10 cr per 100 hull).
- Shield Generator (600 cr + 150 iron + 60 titanium, 30 s, max 1): projects a flattened shield bubble over the whole complex (radius fits the station and every structure plus 70 m, grows when the complex grows; standalone turrets do not stretch it). Ships fly through it. Hostile shots from outside hit the bubble instead of anything inside; hostiles already inside hit normally; friendly shots pass out. 2400 HP, regenerates 30 HP/s after 5 s without hits; at 0 it collapses and re-raises 45 s later at 40%. The generator (hull 900 + 150 shields) can only be shot while the bubble is down; destroyed, the bubble is gone until it is rebuilt.
- The build tooltip and the building panel of both show what they do in numbers (DefenseStats, computed from the scripts and the faction loadouts: damage per second, range against raider gun range, time to kill each raider type, time for 2 or 4 scavengers to break the bubble).

- Science Lab (station addon, 700 cr + 150 iron + 40 titanium, 25 s, max 1): hire researchers and fund projects (see Research).
- Explorer Outpost (700 cr + 140 iron + 30 titanium, 25 s, max 1, hull 1600; model assets/models/buildings/explorer_outpost.glb, 2 inner berths): locked until Jump navigation. Hires explorers (300 cr, max 3, new ship 500 cr) who fly explore contracts through the jump gates (see Gate network). No cargo shuttles.
- Raider Dock (900 cr + 180 iron + 60 titanium, 30 s, max 1, hull 1600): locked until Planetary assault doctrine. Hires landers (300 cr, max 4, new dropship 550 cr) who fly dropships (assets/models/ships/dropship.glb) on planet raid contracts. Runs cargo shuttles like a Mining Outpost (1 to 3, 60 a trip): landers unload the loot into its stock, the shuttles fly it to the station.

## Research
- Tier I extras: Ballistic calibration (Weapons, 250 cr + 30 iron, 1:30: every mercenary +5% hit chance; prerequisite of Fire control computers) and Modular cargo racks (Planetary, 220 cr + 40 iron, 1:30: every miner holds 10% more; prerequisite of Bulk cargo holds). Bonuses are TechDef.merc_accuracy and miner_hold, applied in ShipLoadout.derive and refitted on completion.
- Jump navigation (Engineering, tier I, 400 cr + 60 iron, 1:30): unlocks the Explorer Outpost.
- Forward operations (Engineering, tier II, 900 cr + 150 titanium, 3:00, after Jump navigation): unlocks the Forward Station (see Forward stations: founding).
- Data: data/research/*.tres (TechDef: id, name, field, tier, credits and ore cost, research time, prerequisites, unlocked module ids and building ids, tags for personalities, hidden, description), listed in data/research/catalog.tres (TechCatalog). State in the Research autoload; the Science Lab service (ScienceLab) only drives the work while the lab stands.
- Start set: one Mk I module per slot for each role (miner: mining laser, ore hold, reinforced plating, ion drive, prospector array; mercenary: autocannon, ammo rack, armour plating, combat thrusters, targeting array; salvager: salvage claw, salvage hold, plating, ion drive, wreck scanner). Everything else (shields, survival cells, ejection rigs, all Mk II and Mk III) needs research before the Factory makes it and the Outfitter sells it. Tooling still gates Mk II and Mk III on top. Fitted modules never go away, except one that is not for the ship's role (ShipLoadout.needs): it is sold back at the next Outfitter visit or on load, list price refunded.
- Researchers: hire 1 to 3 at the lab from 3 applicants (new ones every 4 min), 150 cr + 40 per skill point. Each has a field (Engineering, Weapons, Planetary), skill 1 to 10 (rolled 2 to 8) and a personality. They are not ships. Speed: 0.5 + 0.1 x skill (skill 5 = 1.0), x0.6 outside their field. Every second finished project raises their skill by 1. Wage: 2 + 0.5 x skill credits per minute each, paid from your credits (the Staff tab and the hire tooltip show it). When the station cannot pay (no credits) that researcher is "Unpaid" and stops working until it can; a feed line says so. Let go: no refund, their project keeps its progress.
- Indirect control: you fund projects, idle researchers pick among the funded ones nobody works on and keep it until done. Score = field (1.0, outside it 0.55) x personality x resume (x1.3 when started, Methodical x1.6). Ambitious: x(1 + 0.5 per tier above I) x(1 + cost / 1500). Cautious: defensive work x1.8, weapons x0.6. Curious: Planetary x2.2, a new building x1.4. Methodical: tier I x1.5, tier III x0.7, under 600 cr x1.4. The lab log and the researcher tooltip give the reason with the numbers and the runner-up.
- Funding works like a bounty: the credits are reserved at once (shown in the panel and the credits tooltip), the ore is spent; you cannot fund past your credits. The reserve is used up when the project is done. Cancel: all of it back while nobody has worked on it, half the credits and half the ore once started.
- Panel (select the lab): Projects tab grouped by field with state (locked, available, funded and waiting, in progress with who, how far and time left, done), cost and time, Fund and Cancel (the tooltip says what comes back); hovering a project shows what it unlocks with the module numbers. Staff tab: researchers, applicants, lab log. Top bar: a RESEARCH box with the projects funded and the lead one's progress (tooltip lists them; click selects the lab). A finished project posts a feed line naming what it unlocks.
- Module tooltips (Factory warehouse and demand, Outfitter, captain panel, lab) show each module's effect in numbers and "Needs research: X" while locked.
- Old saves without research data keep what they could use: every module tech up to the Factory's tooling tier counts as done, plus the tech of every module a captain has fitted, the warehouse holds or a captain ordered. The Raider Dock tech is not granted.
- Tech tree (time at speed 1; I/II/III = tier):
  - Engineering: Shield emitters I (300 cr + 60 iron, 1:30: shield projector, deflector Mk I), Survival systems I (250 cr + 50 iron, 1:20: survival cell, ejection rig Mk I), Laminated alloys II (plating and armour Mk II), Ion drive tuning II (ion drive, combat thrusters Mk II), Shield lattice II (after Shield emitters), Rescue beacons II (after Survival systems), Ceramic composite armour III, Fusion torch drives III, Phase shield arrays III, Stasis lifeboats III (each after its Mk II tech).
  - Weapons: Rail-assisted cannons II (autocannon Mk II), Linked ammo feeds II (ammo rack Mk II), Fire control computers II (targeting array Mk II), Mass driver cannons III (autocannon Mk III, after rail cannons and fire control), Predictive targeting III (targeting array and ammo rack Mk III, after fire control and linked feeds).
  - Planetary: Deep core drilling II (mining laser, salvage claw Mk II), Bulk cargo holds II (ore and salvage hold Mk II), Orbital survey II (prospector array, wreck scanner Mk II), Planetary assault doctrine II (900 cr + 120 iron + 60 titanium, 3:40, after Orbital survey: the Raider Dock), Plasma bore drilling III, Compression holds III, Deep survey III.
  - After the assault doctrine (C2): Heavy dropships (Planetary III, 1,400 cr: landers carry 40% more per raid), Armoured dropship hulls (Engineering III, 1,300 cr: lander hull x1.4 and +60 shields). Applied at once to every lander (ShipLoadout.derive).
  - Hidden, for C4: Planet scanners (Planetary II).
  - Costs by tier: I 250 to 300 cr + iron, II 500 to 900 cr + iron and titanium, III 1,100 to 1,500 cr + titanium and crystal; times 1:20 to 5:20.

## Planets (C2 and C3 built; C4 planned)
- C2, Raider Dock and planet raids (built). Landers (captain role "Lander", violet in the pilot list, its own chip, specialty Landing) are hired at the Raider Dock and fly dropships (LanderShip, LanderBrain, LanderEvaluator).
  - Select a planet or moon (click it or its row in the overview): the planet panel shows type, loot, base yield (and what the Factory pays for a load now), danger, distance, owner (see C3), raids so far, the contracts on it and "Post planet raid" (pay per load, load limit). Paid per delivery like mining: nothing reserved, posting needs one payment, paused while the station cannot pay.
  - Loot and yield (data/contracts/planet_raid_rules.tres, PlanetRaidRules): rocky heavy ore 44 a load, ocean water 36, desert silicates 34, ice water 48, lava rare metals 18, gas giant fuel 26; times the size (radius / 900, 0.8 to 1.35) and a stable richness per planet (0.85 to 1.25), times the dropship's cargo stat. Fair pay per load: rocky 90, ocean 80, desert 100, ice 90, lava 120, gas giant 120, +20 per danger level above 1. Danger: lava and gas giants 2, others 1, +1 past 15 km and +2 past 30 km from home, at least what nearby bases and clusters give the spot. On the surface 22 to 32 s by type (Landing skill and hold scale it).
  - Choice (LanderEvaluator, logged with the numbers and the runner-ups): pay (Greed), travel time, danger (Nerve, gear relief) like a miner, plus a rich world term from the load's value at the Factory now, times a taste for a big haul (Greedy x2, Bold x1.2, Lazy x0.8, Cautious x0.5), minus crowding. Cautious pilots refuse danger 3+ below 1.6x fair pay; lazy ones want better pay for the far worlds (distance only lowers the score, there is no trip limit; a refusal down to it reads "Not worth the trip at this pay").
  - The flight: undock, cruise (and warp) to an entry point just outside the atmosphere on the side facing home, lifted toward the camera, riding with the planet; a 14 s dive along the curve of the planet with a re-entry glow and an orange trail, the ship shrinking a little into the haze; on the surface with the cargo pod lowered and a dust ring at touchdown; a 12 s climb with a hot engine trail; home. Landed or in the atmosphere a dropship cannot be shot; on the cruise legs raiders hunt it like a miner (it flees, scavengers steal part of the load). Loot goes into the dock's stock and reaches the station by cargo shuttle.
  - Measured (probe, 4 landers, 5 raids posted at fair pay): Khepri (14.8 km, 27 silicates) and Lume (2.6 km, 30 heavy ore) runs take 3 to 4 min, Tavros (26 km, 40 fuel) about 6 to 7 min, a load worth 300 to 800 cr at the Factory's price while its stock is low against 100 to 160 cr pay. 2 of 4 landers were lost to scavengers on the way in 30 min.
  - Every planet keeps a raid record (loads, units, credits, last raid; saved under "planets"), the base for C4 depletion.
- C3, faction claims (built). A faction holds a planet or moon with an orbital garrison (ClaimGarrison, a ScavengerDen subclass in SolarSystem.dens; PlanetClaims under CombatWorld keeps the claims and re-claims). Model assets/models/buildings/claim_garrison.glb (89 m across): its "faction_paint" material is tinted per garrison in the faction colour, the beacon blinks, the four flak turrets swing to their targets (each within 115 degrees of its rest heading, spread over the nearest three attackers) and fire from their muzzles. It rides with its planet at a fixed offset on the lit side (past the atmosphere or rings plus 650 m, clear of moon orbits, 220 m up; Tavros 5.0 km, Noctis 3.4 km from the centre).
  - Numbers (FactionDef "Planet claims"): Corsair garrison 3,400 hull + 900 shields (10/s), flak 4 x 3.0 dmg x 1.3/s at 800 m (was 3.5: four mercs win reliably, three have a fair chance), 2 patrols (respawn 55 s); Syndicate garrison 6,000 + 1,800 (12/s), flak 4 x 4.5 x 1.2/s at 900 m, 2 gunships (90 s). Patrols guard the planet: they roam between its reach and the guard radius (the garrison's distance plus 1.8 km) and attack any of your ships inside it (not shuttles or pods). The garrison sends no raids and does not count for the threat clock.
  - Effects: landers refuse planet raids on a claimed world, before anything else, with the reason "Claimed by Redwake Corsairs, garrison in orbit" (in the decision log too); landers already on their way turn back when a world is claimed. Danger near a garrison rises like near a base (moons of a claimed planet are rougher). The planet panel shows the owner in the faction colour, the garrison's hull and shields, patrols out and their guard radius, a "Select garrison" button (for the bounty), and a pending re-claim; the planet label, its minimap dot (a ring) and its overview row ("held by Redwake") carry the claim.
  - Breaking a claim: a normal bounty on the garrison (escrow, merc fleets; the fleet need counts its flak and patrols). Destroyed: "Tavros is unclaimed: the Redwake garrison was destroyed" and a toast; its patrols withdraw (warp out once clear); the wreck stays 2 min.
  - Re-claim: after FactionDef.reclaim_delay (Corsairs 8 to 13 min, Syndicate 10 to 15 min, +15% per failed attempt), only while the faction has a base in the system (otherwise it checks again each minute). 90 s ahead: "Long-range scans: Redwake is moving to re-claim Tavros, claim group in 1:29" (feed and toast). The claim group warps in 5.5 km from the claim site on the side of the faction's nearest base: a constructor (the garrison folded small, 800 hull + 300 shields for the Corsairs, 1,600 + 500 Syndicate, 85 m/s, unarmed) and 3 escorts guarding it. At the site it deploys for 60 s (a hologram of the garrison fills up; "Faction claim: Redwake is deploying a garrison over Tavros"); then the garrison stands and the surviving escorts become its patrols. Constructor shot down: "Tavros held: the Redwake constructor was shot down by ..." and the faction tries again later.
  - Defending: defence zones can sit anywhere; a zone within 4 km of a planet's or moon's reach rides that body (like zones near the station ride the station), and patrol points inside it stay off the planet. Turrets cannot be built at planets (build zone only).
  - New factions announce themselves with a claim (ThreatDirector, DenDef.claim_planet and claim_lead): the claim comes claim_lead seconds before the base would be due and the base keeps its usual time (at least half the lead later). Redwake (Corsairs) claims Noctis 4 min ahead, Hollowstar claims Orcus 4 min ahead, Obsidian (Syndicate) claims Tavros 5 min ahead. The faction intro and "New faction" toast come with the first claim. Rustjaw and Ashfang claim nothing, so the early game keeps every world open.
  - Measured (probes, stock Mk I mercs, bounty 1,600 cr, garrison at Noctis 38 km out): Corsair garrison, the fleet is called at 3 ships: with 3 mercs hired fails 2 of 2 (one merc lost, garrison left at 17% and 100%), 4 mercs wins 2 of 2 (80 to 150 s of fighting, no losses), 5 mercs 1 of 1 failed (one lost, 10% left), 6 mercs wins 2 of 2 (80 s, no losses). Syndicate garrison (Tavros, 3,000 cr): 4 mercs refuse ("Needs about 5 mercenaries, 4 available"), 6 mercs send 5 and win 2 of 2 (80 to 100 s, no losses).
  - Re-claim (probes, Corsairs at Tavros): undefended the garrison stands again about 2 min after the group arrives (2 of 2); a defence zone (3 km, 150 cr kill bounty) placed at the warning with 4 to 6 mercs stopped it 3 of 3 (the constructor shot down during the deployment); a bounty on the constructor posted at the same time pulled the mercs into a fleet that did not form in time and the re-claim went through (1 of 1).
  - Saves: garrisons are saved with the dens plus the "claims" key (planet, faction, gang, side), claim groups with their phase, constructor (and its progress) and escorts; patrols and escorts keep their guard. Old saves load with no claims until the ThreatDirector makes them.
- C4, depletion and defences: each raid lowers a planet's yield, which recovers slowly. Planet defence batteries shoot dropships (rich planets are dangerous). More research: heavy dropships, armoured dropship hulls, planet scanners that reveal the yield (already in the tech data, hidden). Planet panel: owner, garrison, yield and loot type, recovery, raid history.
- Later maybe: permanent extractors on cleared, held planets.

## Asteroid clusters
- Ore comes from 4 to 6 asteroid clusters (AsteroidCluster, built from ClusterDef). Each is a dense clump 1 to 3 km across (about 1,200 to 9,000 rocks in a few sub-clumps, gaussian heights, thinner at the rim) on a slow inclined orbit around the star (about 12.6 m/s, 0.4 to 2.5 degrees). It carries one main ore (iron, titanium or crystal) and maybe a secondary one.
- Rarer ore lies further out and is riskier: iron inside about 20 km, titanium 16 to 33 km, crystal past 27 km (nothing inside 9.5 km of the star). Base danger: 1, crystal +1, past 30 km +1; living hostile bases within 6 km of a cluster's rim add their danger bonus (max 5). Clusters drift past the fixed bases, so a cluster's danger rises and falls on its orbit.
- Reserve: 25 to 60 loads (bigger and richer hold more). Each drilled load takes one; the rocks thin out visibly as it empties (asteroids shrink away in the shaders, miners skip them). Miners reserve a rock when they set out, so a miner only leaves for a load that is there; one that finds every load taken turns back with a log line. The last load starts a 12 s fade, then the cluster leaves the system.
- Respawn (ClusterSpawner): 3 to 6 min after a cluster runs out (sometimes two, never more than 6), and whenever fewer than 4 are left, a survey finds a new one: an orbit band clear of every planet, its moons and the station orbit, a spot clear of gates, bases and the home station, as far from the other clusters as it gets, the ore the system has least of favoured. Feed line and toast: "Survey signal: new iron cluster near Khepri", named from SystemDef.cluster_names (Talon Shoal, Ember Drift, ...).
- Start set (data/systems/home_system.tres; star 600 m radius, planets Ashra 8.8 km, Verda 13.3 km (home), Khepri 16.1 km, Tavros 24.45 km, Nivis 30.6 km, Orcus 38.1 km, Noctis 46.5 km, gates 50.5 km): Cinder Cluster (iron, 10.5 km, near home: about 1.7 km rim to station at the start), Talon Reach (titanium, 17.9 km, by the Rustjaw Hideout), Crystal Veil (crystal, 34.1 km), Wraith Drift (crystal and titanium, 41.6 km). Hostile bases sit at fixed spots (DenDef.anchor_radius) in the regions they are named for.
- Ships cross a cluster in a lane over or under it only when their way crosses its footprint (AsteroidCluster.lane_altitude clears the rocks wherever the orbit has lifted the clump); other routes fly level.
- Map: clusters are blobs on faint orbit lines on the minimap and labelled clumps in the system view; the cluster panel shows ore, richness, loads left, danger and the bases in reach. Saved: every cluster's def, loads left, and the spawner's due surveys; older saves keep the start set full and map their belt names onto it.

## Contracts
- Mining: on an asteroid cluster, credits per delivered load. No new trips once the cluster has no load left for them; when it is mined out the contract completes as soon as the last trip on it ends (and its linked escort contract is cancelled). Nothing is in escrow, so nothing is refunded.
- Bounty: on a raider ship or a hostile base (Scavenger Hideout, Corsair Nest, Syndicate Fortress), paid on destruction. The suggested pay and the bounty's danger follow the faction. Posting reserves the reward: it leaves your credits at once into an escrow held by the contract board (you cannot post more than you have; the top bar credits tooltip and the overview show "Reserved for bounties"). The payout comes from the escrow (split by rank and damage as before); anything left after the kill (nobody eligible) comes back. Cancel: all of it back while nobody has taken it; once taken, half back and half paid to the takers as compensation, split evenly (the cancel tooltip says which). A raider slipping home without dying refunds it all. One bounty per target: with one posted the target panel's form is "Update bounty" with its reward filled in, and posting again (from any path, ContractBoard.post_bounty) sets that contract's reward: a raise reserves the difference ("Reserved: bounty raised on Rustjaw Hideout +300"), a cut gives it back but only while nobody has taken it (else the minus button is off, "Taken: can only raise"). Takers and the fleet go on, the split at the kill uses the new reward, and the mercenaries forget their refusals of it and judge it again. Feed: "Bounty on Rustjaw Hideout raised to 1,100 cr". Old saves with two bounties on one target load them as one (rewards and escrow added up, takers kept).
- Paid per delivery or trip (mining, escort, salvage bonus): nothing is reserved, but you can only post one while you can pay one payment. While the station cannot pay one payment the contract is paused ("Station can't pay"): captains do not take it until credits come in; a trip already under way is paid what is there and the feed says so.
- Escort: on a miner (or a mining contract), paid to escorts per completed trip. Like Majesty's protect flag. On an escorted mining contract (belt panel "Escorted") miners never fly unescorted: a miner that takes it waits in its berth ("Waiting for an escort") until a mercenary on the escort contract has joined it (within 450 m), then they fly out together at the escort's pace, hop together (the miner hops only with its escorts beside it and free to jump; they jump with it) and the miner holds where it is when they fall more than 650 m behind. An escort lost or gone on the way out (shot down, repairs, recalled) sends the miner home to wait for a new one; one already mining finishes its load and comes home (fleeing as usual), waiting for its escorts on the way when they are near, alone when they are gone. Nobody taking the escort contract means the miners keep waiting: the contract rows and the belt panel say "No escort: 2 miners waiting" (hint: hire mercenaries or raise escort pay). Cancelling the escort contract lets the waiting miners go. The wait and the pairings are saved.
- Planet raid (C2): on a planet or moon, credits per delivered load (see Planets).
- Salvage (stage 3): on a scrap field, a bonus per load on top of the Salvage Yard's pay.
- Explore (P3): on a jump gate (select the gate: the gate panel, "Post explore contract"), at home or in a charted system while you view it. The target is the system behind the gate; it must be uncharted or its survey stale (older than 10 min), and there is one explore contract per target. One explorer takes it. The reward is reserved like a bounty and paid once, when the explorer brings the survey home (the station fee comes back as usual). Fair pay: 250 cr + 200 per jump past the first + 50 per danger level above 1 (the danger guessed from the depth while uncharted: depth + 1). The form picks the gate to leave by (the way in, or another gate of the target) and says that a survey earns nothing by itself. Cancel: all back while nobody has taken it; once taken half back now, the explorer still brings the survey and is paid the other half (the board holds the contract until then). The explorer lost: the contract opens again. In the lists: "Ivo exploring Ostra (12/25 waypoints)", "flying to", "bringing the charts home".

## Defence zones
- The player places up to 3 defence zones ("Defence zone" in the build bar, ZONES group; free). A zone is a centre and a radius (0.8 to 4 km, 200 m steps), drawn as a faint disc with a thin rim (cyan, gold with a kill bounty, pulsing red while raiders are inside) and a ring on the minimap. A centre within 5 km of the station moves with the station on its orbit; one within 4 km of a planet or moon rides that body (C3); elsewhere it stays fixed in space (over a lane; clusters drift through it). Zones are areas ships fly through, so the station keep-outs and structure gaps do not apply; only the limit and the system edge do. Click its label or rim to select it.
- Panel: position, distance from home, mercenaries patrolling it, raiders inside, the radius stepper, the kill bounty (0 to 300 cr per raider killed inside, 10 cr steps) and its budget ("Add 100 cr", "Add 500 cr", "Take back"), what it paid so far, and "Remove zone".
- Kill bounty: paid from the zone's budget. The budget is reserved from your credits like a bounty (it cannot exceed your credits; the top bar and the overview show it as "zone budgets"); what is left comes back when you take it back or remove the zone. A raider killed inside pays the mercenaries who hit it in the last 90 s (and the killer), split evenly, while the budget lasts; turret kills pay nothing. Each payment is a feed line, a toast and a captain log entry; a feed line says when the budget is spent.
- Mercenaries: while any zone exists, idle mercs patrol more often (+20%, +10% more while one pays a kill bounty) and pick where: home 0.6 (Cautious 0.9, Lazy 1.2) against each zone 1, plus its kill bounty (per 40 cr +1, times 1 + Greed/2; Greedy x2, Cautious x0.6, Lazy x0.5), plus 1 (Bold 2) while raiders are inside; zones past 6 km from home: Cautious x0.5, Lazy x0.4, Bold x1.2. The decision log has the weights. In a zone they roam random points inside it, 1.5x as long as a home patrol. Raiders inside a zone within 2.5 km of a merc (or of its outpost, docked) count as threats: patrols engage them, docked mercs scramble. A feed line says when raiders enter a zone.

## Colony alarm
- When a structure (outposts, turrets, the shield generator, the station), the shield bubble or a cargo shuttle is shot, the colony raises the alarm for 25 s after the last hit (ColonyAlarm, the mirror of a raider base's alarm). Every 5 s while it lasts, the free mercenaries within 5 km (docked, resting, repairing with hull enough to scramble, or on patrol / home defence; not bounty hunters or escorts, not those going home for repairs) fly to it, circle it and fight its attackers until the alarm is over. One feed line per alarm says who answered ("Mining Outpost 1 is under attack: Bram Petrov, Kenji Valdez answering"); each merc logs why (attacker, distance, hull).

## Enemies
- Factions (data/factions/*.tres, FactionDef): each hostile base (DenDef) belongs to one. A faction sets its ship model and stats, behaviour (prey, aggression, flee threshold, steal or kill, packs, raid style), base model and toughness, loot and bounty multipliers, colour and names. All of them are hostile to the player and never fight each other. Cluster danger comes from nearby bases.
- Scavengers: junk raider ships from Scavenger Hideouts (bases dug into asteroids) in the mid and outer system, where the richer clusters drift past. They hunt miners, steal cargo, and raid near home now and then.
- Corsairs (outer system, Wraith Drift): fast light raiders from Corsair Nests. They hunt miners and salvagers in pairs over a wide arc, grab cargo and run early. They raid the colony rarely, in pairs, and not before 25 minutes.
- Syndicate (outer system, Wraith Drift): slow, tough gunships from a Syndicate Fortress (twice a big hideout's hull, regenerating shields, its own guns). They patrol their territory, engage mercenaries there and fight to the end; their rare raids (3 to 5 gunships, not before 40 minutes) go for the turrets and the outposts, not the ships. A fortress needs a geared fleet.
- Base defence: a base hit by the player's side raises the alarm ("Rustjaw Hideout is calling its raiders home") for 25 s after the last hit. Its ships within 14 km and same-faction ships of other bases within 6 km come back and fight the attackers (not those already fleeing home with loot); while the alarm lasts it launches below its cap 1.5x faster and every new ship joins the defence. Then they go back to roaming.
- Balance targets (measured in probes): a stock mercenary beats a scavenger 1v1 nearly always; corsairs dodge and run, a third or more get away from a lone merc; a syndicate gunship beats or drives off one stock merc more often than not and loses to two. Rustjaw Hideout falls to 3 stock mercs in about 2 to 3 min, Ashfang Hideout to 3 Mk I mercs, a Corsair Nest to 3 or 4 Mk I mercs (4 to 5 min, often one lost), the Syndicate Fortress to 5 or 6 mercs with some Mk II gear in about 3 to 5 min, with losses and failed runs possible. Faction toughness lives in data/factions/*.tres (FactionDef: base hull, shields, regen and guns, ship accuracy and dodge) and the DenDefs in data/systems/home_system.tres.
- Threat escalation (ThreatDirector, DenDef arrival fields): the game starts with the Rustjaw Hideout only. Then, one at a time: Ashfang Hideout (12 min), Redwake Corsair Nest (25 min, introduces the Corsairs), Hollowstar Corsair Nest (40 min), Obsidian Syndicate Fortress (60 min). A base comes at its time only once the colony has grown (Ashfang 3 captains and 3 structures, Redwake 5/4, Hollowstar 7/5, Obsidian 9/6), and anyway at 1.5x its time; at least 3 min between arrivals. While every base is destroyed the threat clock runs 1.5x; with 3 or more bases standing it runs at 0.35x (arrivals take about 3x as long) and the gap between arrivals is at least 9 min. 75 s ahead a "Long-range scans" warning names the faction and region and the spot shows as "Arriving" with a countdown (hologram, map, overview); the base warps in with the construction look and starts launching. A faction's first base posts a one-time intro. Bases that have not arrived add no cluster danger, bounties, markers or raids; a new base waits 4 min before raiding. The overview gives a vague "Next: unknown signals, ~6 min".
- Raids (RaidDirector): the first at 8 min, then every 600 s falling to 360 s (+-40 s) as the pressure grows (half from game time, full at 45 min; half from colony size, captains plus structures, full at 20). Waves: 1 raider, plus 1 per 18 min, plus 1 per 6 of colony size past 4, up to 6; the first raid has 1 raider, the second at most 2. Faction raid sizes as before (Corsairs a pair, Syndicate 3 to 5). Each raid has an objective, by weight: an outpost 3 (Syndicate 4.5), the cargo shuttles of an outpost 1.5 (3 while one is loaded in flight; not the Syndicate), the station 1 after the first 2 raids (Syndicate 2), turrets 1 for the Syndicate; disabled structures are skipped. 30 s ahead a feed line and a toast warn: "Raid incoming from Rustjaw Hideout toward Mining Outpost 1: 2 scavengers, launching in 30 s"; a base destroyed meanwhile calls it off. Measured before the 8 min / 10 to 6 min timing, in a 50 min run with a colony growing from 8 to 16: 13 raids (the old director sent about 8), at 5, 10, 15, 18, 22, 26, 30 min and then about every 3 to 4 min; 1, 2, 3, 3, 3, 4, 5 raiders, 6 from 40 min.
- Raiders attack in order: the shield bubble while it is up (they never chase targets inside it from outside), then the defense turrets, then the shield generator, then the raid's objective (a structure's hull, the shuttle), then shuttles and ships near home (the Syndicate shoots every structure's hull instead); with nothing left to shoot they loot credits from a structure (15 cr per 8 s each, up to 75, Syndicate double). They flee home below 35% hull.
- Raid outcomes on a Mining Outpost (probe, 3 runs each; "held" = the outpost never disabled):

| Raid | Nothing | 2 turrets + shield (1100 cr) | 2 stock mercs (1200 cr) | Both |

  Defences drive scavengers and corsairs off (they flee at 35% hull, so turrets kill few: 2 of 9 in a 3-raider raid) without losses, while mercenaries kill more but take damage and may be away. The Syndicate needs both.
- Later: the Swarm, rogue drones.

## Captains
The captain is the person. The ship is the hardware.
- Personality (headline): Bold, Cautious, Greedy, Lazy. It sets the starting attributes.
- Attributes 1 to 10, rolled at hire: Nerve, Greed, Diligence, Piloting, specialty skill (Mining or Gunnery), Loyalty.
- Traits (stage 3), earned from events, 2 to 3 max, some fade: Survivor, Shaken, Crystal Hound, Grudge, Lone Wolf, Union Rep.
- Reputation ranks (stage 3) instead of levels: Rookie, Licensed, Veteran, Ace, Legend. A rank unlocks module slots (2 up to 6) and higher module tiers. It does not add raw stats.
- Ship modules (stage 3), made by the Factory and bought by the captain with their own earnings at your Outfitter. Better defense and engine modules make miners take more dangerous clusters (every 3 module tiers there count as one danger level less, at most 2). Personality decides what they buy. Slots: tool (drill / cannons / missiles), cargo or ammo, defense (armor, shields), engines, sensors.
- Career switching (stage 3): a captain can change role, e.g. a veteran Bold miner becomes a mercenary. They need a new ship; experience and traits carry over, the specialty skill starts low.
- Death: a destroyed ship has an eject chance (Piloting, modules). Ejected captains return without the ship and keep their rank. Otherwise they are gone for good.
- Explorers (P3, Explorer Outpost, cyan in the pilot list, specialty Surveying): unarmed and quick (175 m/s, 240 hull, 80 shields), they take explore contracts by pay, jumps and danger (ExplorerEvaluator: pay over 300 cr, the trip there, the survey and back against 900 s, danger by Nerve; Bold take an uncharted system at its depth and like it, +0.15 per jump; Greedy follow the pay; Lazy lose 0.25 per jump past the first; Cautious refuse a charted danger of 3 or more ("Too dangerous for a careful pilot") and uncharted systems more than one jump out ("Unknown space more than one jump out"); the decision log has the numbers). Their survey sweep is 8 x the sensor range (prospector arrays fit) x the Surveying skill (0.85 to 1.2): about 12 km stock. Under fire in a survey they run: the route skips ahead to a waypoint 7 km from the raider and a warp hop spools after 1 s without a hit (a spool is not broken by hits); below 35% hull they break the survey off (the contract opens again, the finds still go home). Destroyed outside home: lost, no ejection.
- Mercenaries defend the area around home on their own, patrol the defence zones, answer the colony alarm within 5 km, attack an enemy claim constructor directly when it is within 2.5 km of them, of their patrol zone's edge or of the alarm site (MercenaryBrain.claim_constructors, logged "Went for the ... constructor"; no waiting for a bounty fleet), take bounty and escort contracts by pay and personality, and go home to repair when damaged. They warp on long legs like miners (3 km or more): out to a bounty, home, inside or toward a far defence zone and to an alarm; a target more than 4 km off is closed on first (a hop to where it will be, dropping out 1.7 km short of a ship or 2.2 km short of a base), the attack run starts inside 3 km. No hop under fire or with a hostile within 1.5 km (tried again once clear); home patrol loops never warp. Fleet wingmen hop with the leader, one left behind hops on its own, and the leader holds short of the target (up to 30 s) until the formation is back together. Under fire from ships (not a base's guns) a mercenary turns on the one doing it the most damage lately, within 3.5 km (solo, in a fleet, escorting, on patrol; escorts count their miner's attackers too); a switch holds 6 s and only flips to an attacker doing clearly more. A fleet focuses the ship hurting the fleet most, and goes back to its bounty target once the attackers are dead or gone. Miners flee when attacked.
- Repairs: hull never regenerates in space (shields do). Docked, a ship is repaired at a cost per hull point paid from the captain's wallet (a full merc repair is about 55 cr); the station keeps 35% as income. Broke captains repair what they can pay for. After a fight a merc goes home to repair below its personality's level (Cautious at almost any damage, Bold and Greedy later, Lazy early); escorts and fleets finish their job first.
- Attribute Teamwork (1 to 10, personality base +-2; Lone Wolf -3, Union Rep +2). A bounty too strong for one ship (hideouts, groups) makes a merc with Teamwork 2 or more (or a Grudge against scavengers) call a fleet of 2 to 6: as many as it takes for the free mercs' combined power to cover the target's need with a margin, 130% by default, by the caller's personality (Cautious 150%, Lazy 130%, Greedy 115%, Bold 100%). The need is in stock mercs: a base counts like a ship's fighting power, the square root of its toughness (hull, shields, plus half of each ship it can field, out or still to launch, dodgers more) times its damage (half its guns plus half its ships'); a ship the mean of its toughness and its fighting power, plus 0.4 times the power of each hostile within 900 m. Measured needs: Rustjaw 2.2, Ashfang 2.9, a Corsair Nest 2.4, the Obsidian fortress 5.5, the Corsair garrison 2.1, the Syndicate garrison 3.7, a lone scavenger 0.55, a gunship 1.5 alone and 2.3 with two others near (a stock merc is 1.0, Mk I 1.26, Mk II 1.55). So at the default margin: Rustjaw 3 stock, Ashfang 3 Mk I or 4 stock, a Corsair Nest 3 Mk I or 4 stock, the Obsidian fortress 6 Mk I, 5 Mk II or a mix with two Mk II, never stock ("Too dangerous for the current fleet"). Until it sets out a fleet takes extra members (up to 6) who like the share; after 2 min short of its margin it still goes when it covers the bare need, else it falls apart ("fleet power 5.1, it needs about 5.5"). Ship bounties take fleets up to 6 too (they were capped at 2). Forming up and on the way a ship shooting at any member is fought by the whole fleet, then it flies on (a wingman under fire was left behind alone). Too few free mercs: the caller refuses ("Needs about 5 mercenaries, 3 available"); even 6 mercs like the hired ones too weak: "Too dangerous for the current fleet". The target panel shows the same estimate and, per merc, its latest refusal of that target with the numbers (kept 2 min). Every refused bounty or fleet invite is one decision log line with the reason and its numbers ("Refused bounty on Rustjaw Hideout (800 cr): needs about 4 mercs, 2 free", "not worth the trip, pay 60 vs wants ~190 (17.4 km, danger 2, Lazy)", "Declined to join Mira Novak's fleet on Rustjaw Hideout (267 cr share): teamwork 5, join chance 80%, said no"); a repeat only counts up until the numbers move (wanted pay by 50 cr, the free mercs, hull by 10%). Others join by Teamwork, personality, share, Grudge, distance and how far the fleet covers the need (chance 22% plus 8% per Teamwork point above 1, Cautious +15%, Greedy -10%, Lazy -5%, +-20% by share, -4% per km to the rally point, +30% when they would complete it; the danger per head weighs 0.6 to 1.3 times by how far the fleet falls short; a no is asked again after 40 s). Measured fights (fixed fleets, 3 runs each): Rustjaw 3 stock 3 of 3 (90 to 110 s), 2 stock 1 of 3; Ashfang 3 Mk I 3 of 3 (65 to 75 s), 2 Mk I 2 of 3, 3 stock 3 of 3 (2 to 4 min); Redwake Nest 3 Mk I 3 of 3 (1 min), 2 Mk I 2 of 3; Obsidian 3 Mk II 0 of 3, 4 Mk II 3 of 3 (100 to 140 s), 5 mixed (3 Mk I, 2 Mk II) 3 of 3 (90 to 130 s), 6 stock 3 of 3 (2:40 to 3:20), no ships lost in any won run; gunship 1 stock 2 of 4, 2 stock 3 of 4. With free choice (natural mercs): 2 Mk II on Obsidian refuse ("needs about 5 mercs, 2 free"), 6 stock form a fleet only under a Bold caller and win 2 of 3 (the third falls apart short of the need), 5 with some Mk II win 3 of 3 (2:50 to 3:30); Bold callers do send 2 Mk I to a Corsair Nest (failed 2 of 3, no loss). Each member fires in 70 to 90% of the engaging time. Earlier measures (stock mercs, fair pay): a Rustjaw bounty with 3 mercs gets its fleet out within 1 min about 3 times in 4 (was about 1 in 3); single scavengers near home are taken by everyone, 20 km out by Bold and Greedy always, Cautious about 60%, Lazy not (at 1.5x fair pay nearly all); stock mercs try the Syndicate Fortress only under a Bold caller and six strong; it forms up off the station, sets out once strong enough (or gives up after 2 min still too weak), flies in formation, focuses fire and retreats together; the leader is picked by rank, then Nerve. The bounty is split by rank and damage done, less for those who left early. Lone wolves (high Nerve, low Teamwork) take such bounties alone, keep it all, and die more often.

## Salvage
Destroyed ships (any side) leave scrap fields. A wreck moves with the frame it was made in: the frame the dying ship flew in (the station's approach, a planet), else the station within 5 km, a cluster within 1.5 km of its rim or a planet or moon within 3.5 km of its reach, whichever is nearest; in deep space it stays fixed. Salvagers, contract markers and the panel follow it (the panel says "Moves with the station" or "Fixed in space"); saves keep the anchor and the offset (older saves are anchored by the same rule on load), and a wreck whose cluster faded stays where it is. Stage 2: scrap fields exist and fade after a while. Stage 3: the salvager role, free salvage paid by the Salvage Yard, optional Salvage contracts as a bonus; scrap is reprocessed into ore the yard sells you, and sometimes gives rare parts.

The step is saved with the game (older saves load with it off); a new game starts it unless the player finished or skipped it once (user://settings.cfg, "Tutorial" in the main menu Settings turns it back on).
- Ledger (L, or click the credits in the top bar; L, Esc or the close button shuts it): every credit change, kept in Economy.ledger (Ledger, last 400 entries, saved with the game). A centred overlay between the left panel and the right column, from under the top bar to above the build bar (the list needs more width than the right column, which already shares its room). Header: credits now, the last 10 sim minutes in / out / net and per minute; bars per category for those 10 minutes (in green right, out red left); then the entries newest first (time, category, label, amount, balance after) with All / In / Out chips. Categories: Factory sales, Grant, Fees, Discovery, Salvage, Repairs, Ship sales, Outfitter, Research refund, Contract pay, Bounties, Escrow, Hires, Ships, Buildings, Research, Zones, Upkeep, Other. Reserving credits (bounties, explore contracts, zone budgets) is one Escrow line out and giving them back one line in; a payout from that escrow is a note line (amount in brackets), not a second spend. Repeats merge: same category and label within 3 s, or within a minute for the steady trickles (Factory sales, fees, grant, wages, repairs, raider looting).
- Event feed: icon and coloured edge per kind, slides in, fades. Toasts are banners: red and held 5 s for raids, amber for scans and new factions, green otherwise.

## Gate network
- Built per new game from a random seed (same seed = same network and systems; saved as the seed, the systems are regenerated on load). Home (Solace) is fixed; 6 to 9 generated systems sit 1 to 3 jumps out.
- Each home gate opens its own branch: Ostra Gate leads to Ostra, Kharon Gate to Kharon, and each branch is a tree from there (every branch reaches depth 2, the network always reaches depth 3). Exactly one cross-link joins the two branches, both ends at depth 2 or 3. At most 4 gates per system. Links are gate pairs: the gate is named after where it leads ("Solace Gate" goes home) and knows the gate it comes out of.
- Danger 1 to 5 per system: depth + rand(-1..1), +1 for about a third of the depth-3 systems, clamped (measured over 50 seeds: mean 1.4 at depth 1, 2.0 at depth 2, 3.2 at depth 3).
- A generated system: a star (class M, K, G, F, A or B with its temperature, size (340 m for a small M to 1,080 m for a B) and light), 3 to 8 planets log-spaced from about 8.5 to 10 km out to 44 km (never inside 8.5 km; one that would touch its inner neighbour's reach is pushed out to 500 m clear, and dropped past 44 km once there are 3), clusters and derelicts past 9.5 km, typed by distance (lava and desert inside, rocky and ocean mid, gas giants and ice outside), moons on inclined orbits (gas giants 1 to 3), orbit periods on home's law; 3 to 6 asteroid clusters clear of the planets, richer deeper (ore mix iron/titanium/crystal 50/35/15 at depth 1, 30/40/30 at 2, 15/40/45 at 3; more richness and up to 1.6x the loads); gates past the outermost body, at least 60 degrees apart, tinted by the danger behind them (blue to red); hostile bases there from the start, by danger (0 to 1 at danger 1 up to 3 to 4 at danger 5; corsairs from danger 2 to 3, a syndicate fortress at danger 5; tougher with danger); 1 to 4 derelicts (spot, kind, loot table, tier 1 to 3; see Payoffs).
- Names come from one pool (data/cluster/name_pool.tres), unique across the network: systems, planets and moons, clusters, bases, derelicts.
- Views: the player can switch the view (top bar system menu, H for home) to home, a system marked known, or one with one of their ships inside. Home always runs live; a remote system runs live only while viewed and for 20 s after, otherwise as data: bases count their launches (the ships appear on the next visit), scrap ages, ships move along waypoint trips at their speed. Remote systems have no station, so nothing is built there and no raids or base arrivals start there (bases there raid home through the gates instead, see Payoffs).
- Gate transit: a ship flies to the gate, dives through the portal (a flash in the portal colour), spends 8 s in the jump and comes out of the far gate; captain, wallet, rank and home outpost stay the same and its berth is kept. A captain shot down outside home does not eject. Explorers use it on their contracts; a debug order sends a mercenary through and back.
- Surveys (P3, Survey): the explorer flies through the gates to the target and flies rings around its star: the orbits of every planet, cluster, base, derelict and gate grouped into bands one sweep wide, one ring per band (clear of the planets and their moons), outermost first, each a full lap plus a step against the orbits so everything on the band meets it; points two sweeps apart (about 25 to 40 waypoints, 5 to 8 min). Every 0.4 s it reveals what lies within its sweep (flat distance): planets with their moons, clusters, bases, derelicts, gates. Then home through the exit gate. Not viewed, the system is data and the same route is walked at the ship's speed with the same reveal (live and data reveal the same set; measured, seed 4001: 12 of 12 objects either way, also with a view switch in the middle and a save in the middle). Passing near a base as data it rolls once per base (10% + 8% per danger bonus + 4% per ship out, corsairs +10%, less for Piloting and speed, 3 to 60%): caught, it loses 30 to 65% of its hull (more for tougher bases) or the captain. Live the raiders really hunt it.
- Finds: "Ivo Kovac found a titanium cluster in Ostra" (feed and toast; gates only in the feed), recorded with the finder and the time (Sector.discoveries) and marked delivered when the survey reaches home: the system is known then ("Survey of Ostra complete", toast), with its last seen time. The finds are paid then (discovery bounties, see Payoffs). A survey goes stale after 10 min: "seen 12 min ago".
- Fog of war: in a remote system only what was revealed shows (3D: bodies, clusters, bases, gates with their labels and orbit lines; the minimap, the ship markers, the overview, picking); raiders and scrap only within 12 km of one of your ships there. A system your explorer is in can be viewed while uncharted and fills in as it is swept. The system menu shows "danger ? (2 jumps out)" while uncharted, "danger 3 · seen 12 min ago" once surveyed; the gate panel shows the same with what was charted.

## Payoffs (P4)
- Discovery bounties (DiscoveryBounty): when an explorer brings a survey home (also the finds of a survey broken off), the corporation pays the station for every new find in it: a cluster 80 cr (rock 60, titanium 110, crystal 150), a base 120, a derelict 60, a gate 100, times 1 + 0.25 per danger level above 1, rounded to 5 cr. The explorer gets a 20% finder's cut on top, into their wallet. Income source "Discovery" (green top bar delta); feed line "Corporate bounty: 4 finds in Ostra, +460 cr (Ivo Kovac +92)"; the survey toast adds "+460 cr bounty (4 finds)". Each find is paid once (Sector.discoveries "paid", saved), so loading a save from before or after the delivery never pays twice. Measured (smoke --explore, seed 4242, Ostra danger 1): 6 finds, 560 cr to the station, 112 to the explorer, paid once (also with a save and load before the delivery and another after it).
- Derelicts (Derelict, DerelictLoot): built in a live remote system from their DerelictDef (a burnt, tumbling ship or station model by kind with drifting plates and a blinking red distress beacon; hidden by the fog until revealed; "(STRIPPED)" once looted). An explorer that sweeps one nobody has stripped breaks off its route, flies to it and hovers 10 s, then takes its loot (as data: the same trip at its speed plus the 10 s). The roll is seeded by the network seed, the system and the hulk, so live and data give the same: credits (probe 40 to 90 x tier, freighter 20 to 60, frigate 60 to 120, station hulk 150 to 250), ore (probe crystal, freighter its cargo: titanium or iron, crystal deeper; frigate titanium; station iron and titanium) and planet goods, and a rare Mk II (tier 3: half the time Mk III) module part (probe 15% + 10% per tier, freighter 10% per tier, frigate 25% + 15%, station 45% + 15%). The hold takes 60 units of ore and goods, most valuable first (the rest stays behind, said in the feed); credits and parts take no room. Feed "Ivo Kovac salvaged a derelict freighter in Ostra: 30 crystal, Armor Plating Mk II, 40 cr". The hulk is marked looted at once (RemoteSystemState.looted) and never gives twice; the loot rides home in the survey and lands at the Explorer Outpost with it: ore and goods into the stock, credits as income ("Salvage"), a part into the Factory warehouse (it can make it early, or the next one is cheaper), sold for half its price without a Factory. An explorer lost loses the loot. Measured (probes, seed 4242): the same hulk gave the same loot surveyed as data and live (Ostra viewed the whole time), a second survey of Ostra later took nothing more.
- Cluster map (ClusterMap, M or the top bar's MAP button; Esc or M closes, nothing pauses): full screen in the HUD look. Home and every charted system, and behind each charted gate the system it leads to as a "?" node until surveyed; columns by jumps from home, lines for the gate links (dashed toward the uncharted). A node: the danger colour and number (grey "?" uncharted), name, "danger 3 · seen 12 min ago" or "danger ? (2 jumps out)", stale surveys dimmed, a short contents line (clusters, bases, wrecks left), a cyan badge with your ships there, an amber flag for an open explore contract, a red pulsing ring while a warned gate raid forms there; dots on a link for ships in the jump. Click a node: the info panel (the gate it is behind, danger and survey age, clusters by ore, bases, derelicts left, your ships there and in the jump, the explore contracts, a forming raid), "View system" (when the view may go there) and "Post explore contract (N cr)" at fair pay on the charted gate that leads there (disabled with the board's reason otherwise). Ship markers moved to N.
- Gate raids (GateRaidSource, home's CombatWorld beside the RaidDirector; den raids go on as before): systems at most 2 jumps out with danger 3 or more and a standing base can raid home through the gate on their way (generated depth-1 systems stay at danger 2 or less, so it is the systems behind them, through Ostra Gate or Kharon Gate). From 30 min (plus up to 3 min), every 12 to 20 min, each such system rolls (40% at danger 3, +15% per level above, x0.75 two jumps out); a hit forms a raid of 2 to 5 ships (danger - 1, +0 to 1; a faction's own raid size, the Syndicate 3 to 5) from one of its bases, out 60 to 90 s later. They come out of the home gate's portal (a flash), pick an objective like a den raid (RaidDirector.pick_objective) and fight like normal raiders; below their flee level or done they fly back into the portal and jump out, counted back into their base over there (its pending launches, while that system is data). Their stand-in base is a GateRaidDen at the gate (not a base: no bounty, marker, threat clock or den raids). Warning only when the source is scouted (surveyed in the last 15 min, or one of your ships is in it or jumping there; also when scouting starts while it forms): feed and toast "Raid forming in Corvus, through Ostra Gate in ~1 min" and a THREATS row ("THROUGH THE GATES": the forming raid with its countdown, the raids in progress and their ships). Unscouted they just arrive ("Unknown raiders came through Ostra Gate"). Saved with the system ("gate_raids": the clock, the forming raid, the raids out with their stand-in bases, the tally). Rolls are seeded by the network seed and the check count. Measured: smoke --long (seed 3, sources Corvus behind Ostra Gate and Fennick behind Kharon Gate) 1 raid in 40 min, 31:49 through Ostra Gate, 3 scavengers, 2 jumped back, 1 destroyed; probe (Ostra forced to danger 3 with a base): the first check at 31 min formed one unscouted (no warning), Ostra surveyed a minute before: warned 77 s ahead; against one mercenary all came back out of the gate.

## Forward stations (F0 to F6 built)
- A forward station is a second (or third) station in another system: at most 2, one per system. The system it is in stays live for good (pinned) and simulates at full rate while the view is elsewhere. It is founded by flying one there (founding, below); ForwardStations.debug_found founds one at once for tests.
- Each station has its own buildings, berths and contracts. Captains work their own system's contracts only and stay bound to the station that hired them. A forward station has no Factory; its Outfitter sells from home's Factory warehouse.
- Threat: the station's own system raids it. The first raid comes 10 min after the founding (plus the 30 s warning); after that the raid clock, pressure and wave growth count from the founding, not from the game start. Only the bases already in that system raid: no new base arrivals there. The first wave is sized by the system's danger (1 raider at danger 1 to 2, 2 at 3 to 4, 3 at 5), then grows like home's. Defence zones and the colony alarm work there as at home: mercs hired at a forward Mercenary Outpost answer its alarms and patrol its zones; home's mercs stay home. Miners flee to their own station. The station and its structures take hull damage, go disabled and are repaired like home's.
- Buildable there: every structure except the Science Lab, with the normal per-type limits (Mining Outpost, Mercenary Outpost, Salvage Yard, Explorer Outpost, Raider Dock, Defense Turret up to 8, Shield Generator), defence zones, and on the station's 2 bays the Outfitter or its own Factory (an addon now, 1,200 cr + 200 iron + 40 titanium; without one the Outfitter sells from home's Factory warehouse). Home only: the Science Lab and the Forward Station itself (BuildingDef.allowed_forward; forward_max_count is no longer used by any structure).
- Upkeep: free for the first 2 min after the founding, then 40 cr per sim minute per station, paid every 15 s (ledger Upkeep, "Upkeep: <station>"). An installment the credits cannot cover is owed; while anything is owed, nobody can be hired there ("Upkeep unpaid (N cr owed): no hiring here until it is paid"), and every installment tries to pay the whole debt. Feed lines when it goes unpaid and when it is paid.
- Water: each station uses 2 water per sim minute from the global stock (taken every 15 s). When there is too little it runs dry: nobody can be hired there and its captains work at 0.7x speed (mining, salvage and dock repair timers) until water is back. One feed line when it runs dry, one when it is back; no arrears. The Factory's auto-sell keeps at least 10 min of all stations' water use in stock (more than its own reserve when that is lower; the Factory panel marks it with *). Replacement ships are blocked there like hiring while upkeep is owed or the water is out.
- Hauling fee: 15% of the ore a cargo shuttle lands at a forward station (rounded per type) is kept by the haulers. The ledger gets a note line in category Hauling, "Hauling fee: 3 titanium at Vela Station", with its worth at the Factory's current price in brackets; no credits move. A note rather than an ore-only line because the ledger counts credits: the note keeps the ledger's sum check exact and still shows the fee next to the credit lines it is weighed against.
- HUD (F5): the build bar, placement and defence zones work in any system with a station (the viewed one's buildings, its build zone and bays); only allowed tiles show, with that station's limits. Overview SYSTEM tab: the station row (name, bays) with Upkeep and Water rows under it, then that station's structures. A click on a forward station opens its station panel (hull, bays, upkeep paid/owed, water use and time left, hauling fee, its buildings). The pilot list and the contract strip list every station, home first, others tagged "[Ostra]"; a click on one switches the view there. Feed and ledger lines about another system get the same tag. System menu: stations marked with the station icon; Shift+1 home, Shift+2/3 the forward stations. Cluster map: station icon and name on the node, a station line in the info panel. Top bar water: always shown with forward stations, tooltip lists each station's use and how long the stock lasts, a red "WATER n MIN" while it lasts under 5 min.
- Founding (F6): research Forward operations, then build the Forward Station at home like a big structure (2,500 cr + 400 iron + 150 titanium + 200 water, 1:00; the build bar allows as many as there are free station slots: 2 minus the stations, the ones on their way and the ones under construction). Finished, the site becomes a mobile station parked beside the complex (the forward station model pushed by the colony ship hull as its drive tug; hull 5,000, no shields, 90 m/s, never fights; raiders of every faction hunt it, at home a hit raises the colony alarm).
- Destination: select it ("Set destination" in its panel opens the cluster map) and pick a system with "Set destination here" (charted, danger 4 or less, no station, no other forward station headed there): the view goes there and every planet gets a ring on the would-be station orbit, green where a station may go, red with the reason on hover (a base within 6 km of the planet, rings across the orbit, no clear orbit between the moons). A click on a green one sets it and posts an escort contract on the station (the escort ward form, 200 cr per escort, up to 3, raise or lower the pay in the panel; nothing is reserved). It waits ("Waiting for an escort"; the contract row says "No escort: Forward Station 1 is waiting", hint "Hire mercenaries or raise escort pay") until a mercenary is within 600 m, then leaves. "Cancel destination" (only before it leaves) cancels the contract.
- Flight: through the gates along the shortest route (GateTransit). It holds while an escort falls more than 1.5 km behind and warps only with every escort within 900 m (they hop with it). The escorts near it go through each portal with it (they ride in its save dict and come out of the far gate beside it). Not viewed, the convoy is data: the gate trips as data, and every system it enters as data rolls one ambush (6% per danger level + 6% per standing base, -8% per escort, at most 60%): a hit costs the station 8 to 30% of its hull (more with danger) and each escort 15 to 45% (an escort keeps at least 10%); at 0 the station is lost. Viewed, raiders and base ships really attack it and the escorts fight them.
- Arrival: in the target system it flies to the picked planet (the site point on the station orbit, the angle chosen so it faces the station on arrival; the last 4 km flown in the planet's moving frame), deploys 8 s with a flash and becomes the station (ForwardStations.found: pinned, StationSupply, upkeep free for 2 min). The escorts are paid then (each its pay, as one escort trip) and fly home through the gates.
- Lost on the way (destroyed live or by an ambush as data): a feed line, nothing refunded, the escort contract ends. Saved and loaded in every state (parked, waiting, in transit live or as data, arriving): its own save kind, the escorts riding in its dict, the contract kept on the board meanwhile (it links to the station again by save id). Measured (smoke --colony, seed 24): built in 1:05, left 0:04 to 0:14 after the order, Ostra live in about 3 to 5 min, Gorran (3 jumps, as data) in about 5 to 8 min, both founded, reloads identical.
- A forward captain who dies frees the berth for a new hire, as at home; one who ejects waits at the station for a new ship.
